## Overview

Cyclic is a Notion-style knowledge base for one person: nested pages, a block editor, page icons and covers, a search command, a trash, and a switch that turns any page into a public URL. It ran from November 2023 to March 2024 and shipped in the first quarter of 2024.

The private side needs an account. The public side does not: a published page opens read-only at `/preview/<id>` for anyone with the link, and unpublishing takes it back.

## Outcome

- A page tree, not a flat list: every page can hold children, and archiving a page archives everything under it.
- An editor that saves as you type, with covers, icons, and the usual block types.
- One switch per page: private draft or published link, with a copy button and a way back.
- A search command across pages, a trash that can restore or delete, and sign-in only where it is needed.

## Solution

The workspace is a small data model. One `pages` table holds the title, owner, parent, content, cover, icon, and two flags: published and archived. Two indexes do the work — by owner, and by owner and parent — so the sidebar is one query per level and archiving a subtree is a recursive walk rather than a schema of its own.

The editor loads on the client only, because it is the page. BlockNote provides the block model; the document is stored as the editor's own JSON and written back on every change through Convex's live queries, so there is no save button to forget.

Clerk owns the private routes. The public route sits outside the authenticated shell and reads only a published page; a page that is not published does not resolve there.

## Ideation

It began as a Notion clone for my own notes. The clone is the wrong frame for the whole product — databases, relations, collaboration — but it is a good frame for the part I actually used: a tree of pages that can be written in and, when it matters, shared.

The interesting boundary turned out to be public and private, not rich text. Editors are solved; deciding what a stranger can open should be explicit, per page, and reversible. That became the one piece of product design in the build.

## Reflection

A clone is defined by what it leaves out. There is no collaboration, no comments, no databases, no backlinks — and the app is better for it, because each of those would have doubled the surface without changing how I write.

Live queries made the editor feel local; the cost is that the backend is not mine to move. If I rebuilt it, I would keep the page tree and rethink publishing at a finer grain than a whole page.
