## Overview

DabMusic is where my music lives under my own roof: a static site and player for a fixed catalogue. Albums, audio, artwork, and lyrics sit in one folder on disk; the build reads that folder and prerenders every page, so there is no database, no CMS, and no account. 16 albums, 46 tracks, and lyrics for 34 of them.

The home page is two shelves, unreleased and released. Every album opens on its own page, which paints itself in a tint taken from its cover. An artist view lives at `/dabaz`, and `/design` documents the foundation the site is built on.

## Outcome

- Every release in one place, unreleased included; the platforms still handle distribution.
- One player for the whole site — a mini bar, a full-screen view, and a queue — with the track, position, volume, shuffle, and repeat mode surviving a reload.
- Lyrics that follow the record word by word, with ad-libs on their own line and a line-level fallback where the timing does not exist.
- A site that installs and reloads offline, with per-album share images, a sitemap, and robots.

## Solution

The catalogue is the build. Each album is a folder with an `album.json`; a server-side scan turns those into typed albums and tracks, reads durations, and fails the build when a cover, an audio file, or a lyric file is missing. Adding a release means adding a folder, not filling in a form.

One client owner holds the single `<audio>` element. The mini bar, the full-screen player, and the queue all read from it, so playback survives navigation; every two seconds it writes the track, position, volume, shuffle, and repeat mode to local storage and restores them on the next visit.

Lyrics are the reason the site exists in this shape. Stock LRC can time a line, not a word, and has no room for the ad-libs that sit under one. So the site reads DMLL, a small superset of LRC: word-level spans, `[ad:]` lines, and a left/right voice switch. The full-screen view sweeps a bright mask across the current line, lifts each word as its time arrives, keeps ad-libs dim until they belong, and collapses to the plain line highlight under reduced motion. The waveform beneath the cover doubles as the seek bar.

## Ideation

The first version could have been a page of platform embeds. It would have taken an afternoon, and it would have handed the design to five different players, each with its own idea of what a track looks like. It would also have left the unreleased work — the part no platform carries — outside the page.

The other extreme was a streaming service: accounts, uploads, rights, royalties, a database. That is a business, and the platforms already run it.

What was left was a player for one catalogue, owned end to end. The lyrics came from the same instinct: the available formats could not say what the records do, so I wrote the smallest one that could, and kept it plain text.

## Reflection

The word timings are mostly estimated. Converting 32 line-level LRC files to DMLL gave every track a sweep, but only one was tuned by ear; refining the rest is slower than building the player was, and it is the work that remains. Eighteen files still carry text without line timing, so they can be read but not followed.

The queue works and has no surface of its own yet. Search waits until the shelves outgrow a screen. Both are honest gaps, not missing features: the catalogue is still small enough to browse.

Maintenance is the part I underestimated. Every release means artwork, a folder, lyrics, a waveform, and a tint — and the site is only as current as the last folder I added.
